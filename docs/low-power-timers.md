# AWT and LPTIM run-mode timers

The HAL provides source-reviewed, owned polling and interrupt-driven run-mode timers on all thirteen currently supported families. The peripheral names AWT and LPTIM do not establish a low-power system path.

| Families | Driver | Implemented source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A030, F002, F003, F020, F030, L031, R031, W031 | `awt::Awt` | Undivided factory HSIOSC |
| Exactly F020C6U7, F030C8T7, A030C8T7, L031C8T6/C8U6/F8U6, R031C8U6, W031R8U6 | `awt::Awt` | Owned, initialized, board-qualified LSE |
| L010, L011, L012, L052, L083 | `lptim::Lptim` | Qualified PCLK |

Both drivers follow the pinned Embassy ownership boundary: `Peri`, sealed instance traits, direct typed PAC registers, generated typed GLOBAL interrupts, and central `RCC_INFO`. The executable examples use the actual pinned Embassy thread executor. Their structure is informed by Embassy commit `f16efeffe37581092ec184718e6fdb1620393214`, but CW32 commands, flag polarity and update synchronization are independently reviewed rather than copied from STM32.

## Clock and ownership contracts

`hal::init` must first initialize a supported RCC configuration. Existing AWT `new` and `new_async` select **HSIOSC**, before the HSI divider; its frequency must not be substituted by `Clocks.hsi` or PCLK. On the exact eight above, `new_lse` and `new_async_lse` instead consume a genuine `rcc::LseClock` acquired from the initialized source, SYSCTRL and actual oscillator pads. They observe source readiness before touching AWT or its gate. AWT does not start or retune LSE. The full board, monitor and ownership conditions are in [owned LSE AWT](lse-awt.md).

For either AWT source the prescaler is 2..32768, encoding zero is reserved, and the required MD field is `11`. The period is ARR+1 counter ticks. Configuration completes while EN=0; EN rising reloads ARR. `set_config` deliberately stops the timer, retains its selected source, updates the divider/reload, clears old events and leaves it stopped.

LPTIM selects PCLK with no input/trigger pins, no filter, no encoder, and immediate reload update. Its prescaler is 1..128. Software conservatively rejects ARR=0. `set_config` stops the timer, configures CFGR while EN=0, enables the clock domain, clears stale ARROK, writes ARR once, and waits for ARROK before stopping again. New writes never overlap an unacknowledged update. A synchronization timeout stops the core and faults the object; drop and reconstruct a reborrowed peripheral to recover with an independently owned LPTIM reset.

HSIOSC clock bounds come from the existing generated, own-family electrical data in `cw32-data/electrical.yaml`. HSIOSC AWT uses `ClockBounds::hsi(1)` divided by its actual prescaler; owned-LSE AWT instead divides the held `LseClock`'s root bounds. LPTIM divides `bus_clock_bounds` by its actual prescaler. Rational endpoints are preserved, without deriving bounds from rounded hertz or repeatedly dividing an already-divided AWT tick. No new tolerance percentage or synthetic LSI/LSE bound is introduced. These contracts retain the source's declared VDD, ambient-temperature, factory-trim or board per-cycle qualifications; they do not measure frequency or certify healthy counting after source loss.

The LPTIM EN startup delay covers two counter-clock periods using conservative CPU-upper/counter-lower frequency endpoints. The calculation retains the exact rational endpoints, including valid counter rates below 1 Hz, rather than dividing by a rounded whole-hertz value. This is a relative cycle wait and does not add an absolute-period guarantee to rate-only sources. Its software-start latency is additionally documented as three kernel clocks. `start` and `start_once` restart CNT through acknowledged SRST; they do not promise divider-phase alignment or an exact wall-clock deadline. The driver exposes clock bounds and raw reload configuration rather than claiming an unsupported monotonic/deadline contract. Counter reads use a bounded search for two equal consecutive samples; a fast PCLK/div1 timer may legitimately return `UnstableCounter`.

AWT never pulses reset or disables a clock gate. LPTIM constructor reset is permitted by `RCC_INFO` only for an independently owned reset field, checked against generated shared-group metadata. Drop stops the owned timer, disables only its own event enable while stopped, and leaves every oscillator and bus gate alive. No shared BTIM/ADC/AWT group is reset or gated. The HSIOSC AWT and PCLK LPTIM paths retain their frozen RCC contract; LSE AWT additionally retains SYSCTRL and source-pad ownership inside its `LseClock`. Neither token ownership nor a frozen clock declaration prevents physical source failure.

Acquiring or reacquiring that `LseClock` uses the existing GPIOC pad inspection, which may temporarily open an originally gated whole bank and advance retained filters/armed edges, even before an acquisition error. Restoring the gate does not undo that progress. AWT's admission and `is_clock_ready()` do not inspect pads or open GPIO banks. On L031/R031/W031, GPIOA/B/C/F `FILTER.FLTCLK=7` selects standalone AWT underflow. Takeover, configuration/EN changes, stopping, Drop or source loss can change or stall retained filtering and external reactions; forgetting can leave them driven. F020/x030 FILTER tables do not qualify that value. No new recipient scan or all-recipient-quiescence precondition is added.

Forgetting an AWT skips Drop and may leave counting and IRQs active; the static IRQ state contains no pointer into the forgotten object. Reborrowed tokens may later be reused. Successful reconstruction stops/replaces local configuration and clears old events, whereas a failure before local writes preserves them. LSE, monitor LSI, faults and pad reservations remain retained. An independently owned live `Rtc` cannot simultaneously consume the same `LseClock`; dropped/forgotten RTC hardware may still use LSE and is left untouched.

## Commands and interrupts

- AWT ISR is read-only; ICR.UD is R1W0. Its documented reset/no-op seed is `0x3f`, including reserved reset-one bits. Acknowledge changes only UD from that seed.
- LPTIM ISR is read-only; ICR has seven R1W0 event flags. Its `0x7f` no-op seed preserves all other events when acknowledging ARRM or ARROK.
- LPTIM CR/CR0 contains mixed commands: SNGSTART/CNTSTART are write-one commands; SRST/ARST are write-zero reset commands with ready readback. Its generated `0x18` no-op seed avoids accidental reset or replay of a start command. The driver waits for reset readiness before issuing SRST and waits for acknowledgment afterward.
- LPTIM IER is writable only while EN=0. The handler never changes it while counting. It clears ARRM, latches one coalesced software event and wakes the task.
- L052 shares `BTIM2_LPTIM` and L083 shares `BTIM2_LPTIM1`. Neither constructor, handler, cancellation nor drop disables/unpends the NVIC line. All other enabled sources on the vector require their own handler, including bootloader/raw-PAC owners. A foreign unhandled source may otherwise cause an interrupt storm.
- `wait().await` consumes an already-retained event. Cancelling that future leaves the timer and event intact. Multiple elapsed periods can coalesce into one event; this is not an event counter. Stopping retains an event; restarting deliberately discards it. Dropping the timer stops its hardware, but cancelling a wait does not.
- On the admitted LSE AWT cohort, `is_clock_ready()` samples the selected source's existing readiness predicate. It neither clears an event/fault nor supplies continuous monitoring or a fault wake. `take_elapsed()` and `wait()` retain their event semantics even after source loss. A retained event may predate a fault; with no event, `wait()` may remain pending indefinitely. `is_running()` reports EN and `counter()` samples CNT; neither proves progress or elapsed wall time. A deadline needs an independently waking valid source. Local register access still requires functioning CPU/PCLK, gate and reset domains.

Register enums and command seeds are curated in `cw32-data/registers/{awt,lptim}_*.yaml` and `cw32-data/register-writes.yaml`, projected through the normal data/PAC generators. Current canonical AWT inputs use `awt_cw32f030_v1` for F020/F030/A030 and `awt_cw32l031_v1` for L031/R031/W031, both with LSE=3. F002/F003 retain `awt_v1`, where 2/3 are distinct HEX pad inputs and no LSE selector is present. This is an existing IP split, not a new common-source enum.

IRQ and RCC routes are consumed from existing generated chip metadata. [The original timer evidence](low-power-timers-evidence.json) remains byte-for-byte historical, including its old F020/F030/A030 `awt_v1` labels. [The current LSE AWT proof](lse-awt-evidence.json) records this extension's own source/data facts and current IP mapping without rewriting that record.

## Remaining dependencies

The exact-eight LSE AWT path reuses the existing qualified board/source capability; it does not qualify LSI, HSE/HEX AWT counting, other packages or low-speed LPTIM operation. Those modes still require their own electrical, readiness, source-lifetime and retained-consumer contracts. HSI bounds must never stand in for a low-speed oscillator. A nominal LSE frequency or average ppm alone does not establish the board's required per-cycle bounds.

Deep-sleep wake further requires a source-reviewed system entry/resume sequence, wake-enable and pending-flag handling, retained clock/voltage/flash transitions, peripheral stop constraints, and a race-free executor sleep/wake path. An Embassy time driver requires persistent monotonic epoch/overflow accounting, alarm scheduling, rollover/compare race handling and a reviewed time queue. Neither is supplied by these timer APIs. External counting, GPIO routing, triggers, encoder and PWM are also not implemented.

## Verification

Historical HSIOSC/PCLK verification used ordinary ARMv6-M checks and the polling/interrupt firmware examples. Its original evidence and receipts retain their scope. The new `lse_events` example uses a bounded-iteration Blocking phase, Drop/reacquisition and then a continuing typed-IRQ Async phase; the iteration bound promises neither a duration nor arrival of an event.

Stage80 main produced 11 ordinary ARM libraries and 8 real ELFs with zero warnings: a successful 11-library prefix followed by an 8-ELF continuation on identical source/toolchain. The first build assertion failed before producing any artifact; after its RCC-name correction, the first ELF attempt exposed a missing locked offline dependency. Official locked fetching preserved the lockfile, and only the ELF tail resumed. Those failures remain in the receipt history; the result is not a single uninterrupted successful 19-row run. Generation, the whole54/905 zero-delta comparison and all four finite source/data gates passed separately. Independent runtime and metadata/source reviews accepted the frozen main implementation.

At main acceptance clean had not run; final completion requires a separate final-source 2-library/2-ELF clean receipt. The cumulative local recipe is not expanded or counted as execution. No host MMIO model, HAL test/probe or firmware execution supplies runtime evidence. Compile/link and source review cannot establish source-loss behavior, detector latency, cancellation races, elapsed-time accuracy or silicon clock-domain timing. See [the LSE AWT acceptance boundary](lse-awt.md#verification-boundary).
